Line Drill

Learn your lines one cue at a time.

Paste your script and pick your character. Line Drill shows each cue, reads the other parts aloud if you like, and hides your line until you’ve had a go. It remembers which lines you know and which need work.

Your script stays on this device. Nothing is uploaded.

Works best when each speech starts with the name and a colon, like ROSA: Thirty minutes?. Text copied from a PDF with each character’s name on its own line usually works too.

Script & character

Each character keeps their own progress, so switching back picks up where you left off.

Each speech starts with the character name and a colon, like ROSA: Thirty minutes?. Stage directions sit on their own line in parentheses. # 78 marks a page and @ Diner, Day 1 starts a scene. Fixing a line keeps the history of every other line.

Listen for my lines

After each cue, Line Drill turns on the microphone and listens while you say your line. When you’ve said it, it moves on to the next cue by itself. If you stop partway and pause, it shows the line, marks the words it didn’t hear, and counts the line as called.

Where your voice goes. Your browser does the speech recognition, not Line Drill. In most browsers that means your voice is sent to the company that makes the browser, such as Google for Chrome or Apple for Safari, to be turned into text, under their privacy policy. Some devices do it on the device instead.

Line Drill itself never records or uploads anything. The text the browser sends back is compared with your line on this device and then thrown away.

The microphone is only on while the Listen button is lit. Tap it again to turn listening off.
